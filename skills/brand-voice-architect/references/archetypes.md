# Anchor Archetypes and Axis Map

Four archetypes derived from published guides of real brands (Mailchimp, Apple,
Dropbox, Motive). Use them as anchors for locating a brand, never as boxes. Most
brands land between two.

## The Warm Expert (model: Mailchimp)

Teacher-companion who has been where the reader is. Educates without patronizing,
values being liked, humor is dry and self-deprecating. Voice is constant; tone
modulates by the reader's emotional state, and clarity always outranks
entertainment. Mechanical signature: company as "we," contractions everywhere,
plain words, jokes that bring the reader in.

Fits: SMB SaaS, HR and payroll, consumer fintech, tax software, healthcare
navigation, education. Any category where the reader arrives anxious and the brand
wins by lowering the temperature.

## The Invisible Documentarian (model: Apple's style guide)

Not a marketing voice: a consistency machine for instructional, reference, and
interface text. Personality is treated as a liability; precision and terminological
consistency are the entire value. Informal only in mechanics (contractions yes,
Latin abbreviations no).

Fits: documentation and UI layers of any company, developer platforms, medical
devices, regulated enterprise. Most companies need this voice alongside their
marketing voice, and the two should be documented separately.

## The Plainspoken Utility with a Spark (model: Dropbox)

Simple, helpful, human, plus a permitted flash of wit. Compressed and utility-first;
the pillars are additive (a line should be all four at once). Freshness test: if the
writing doesn't surprise the writer, it won't surprise the reader. Thinnest
framework of the four; strength is memorability, weakness is enforcement.

Fits: productivity tools, consumer apps, DTC services. Categories where the product
is used daily and the brand wins by being frictionless and likable without being
chatty.

## The Credible Operator (model: Motive)

Momentum and competence; warmth and humor explicitly banned ("smart, not warm").
Short punchy sentences, inventive verbs, relentless economy. Intensity dials up and
down by audience (investors low, billboards high) rather than by reader emotion.
Company may refer to itself as "it."

Fits: B2B industrial, logistics, cybersecurity, fintech infrastructure, commercial
insurance. Categories where the buyer is skeptical, stakes are operational, and
friendliness reads as unserious.

Caution: this archetype's native devices (fragment stacks, triads) collide with
AI-tells gates. If a brand locates here, name the collision in Phase 2 and record
the resolution.

## The Six Axes

1. **Warmth:** banned ↔ low ↔ central. Low-mid usually means warmth through
   usefulness rather than sentiment.
2. **Humor:** none ↔ rare-dry ↔ open spark. Also capture surfaces where humor is
   banned regardless of setting (failure states, billing, product copy are common).
3. **Reader relationship:** authority ↔ guide ↔ peer. Watch for the fake-peer
   failure (manufactured casualness) as a named anti-model.
4. **Energy:** calm ↔ driven. Calibrates sentence rhythm and verb choice.
5. **Self-reference:** "it" ↔ "we" ↔ "I". One pronoun rule that encodes the whole
   warmth axis. Solo founders and studios need this asked explicitly.
6. **Register split:** one voice ↔ distinct registers by deliverable. Most product
   companies split product copy (compressed) from long-form (roomier); capture each
   register's ceiling separately.

## Guide-type spectrum (for scoping the output)

Pillars-only (Dropbox) → pillars + house style + medium playbooks (Mailchimp) →
plus audience dials and channel templates (Motive) → pure terminology dictionary
(Apple). Ask the user how far down this spectrum their guide needs to go; default
to the Mailchimp point.
