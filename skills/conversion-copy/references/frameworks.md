# Framework Mechanics

Two sections: argument frameworks (Axis 2 — chosen by hook shape) and surface patterns (Axis 1 — chosen by asset type). Read only what the current asset needs.

Universal rule for both: the framework organizes messages; the VOC supplies them. A framework filled with guesses produces organized guesses.

---

## Argument frameworks (Axis 2)

### PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

The default for problem-shaped hooks. The most reliable direct-response structure in existence.

1. **Problem.** State it in the customer's words, near-verbatim. One problem. Category-level for cold audiences.
2. **Agitate.** More examples of the same pain and adjacent pains — bring it to life, don't repeat it. Agitation is *specific instances*: "it takes five seconds to open the door," "you walk away not knowing if it's locked," "I have to dig through menus to play a song." Pull these from the VOC problem bucket. Two to four instances; stop before it reads as piling on.
3. **Solution.** The offer, framed as the direct answer to the agitated instances. If agitation was "menus and touchscreens," the solution language is "buttons" — mirror the specifics, don't abstract them.

Tone check: PAS requires the brand to be able to afford an agitation register. If the brand can't, soften step 2 to examples without twist-the-knife energy.

### DOS — Desire, Obstacle, Solution

For desire-shaped hooks ("I wish this were true").

1. **Desire.** The thing they want most, stated in their words.
2. **Obstacle.** What's in the way of getting it — from the VOC, not invented.
3. **Solution.** The offer as the remover of that specific obstacle.

Choosing PAS vs. DOS is not data-driven at the margin; it's read from the hook. Problem hook → PAS. "I wish" hook → DOS. Either can work; the hook says where to start.

### ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

For audiences who don't yet know the problem exists (cause marketing, category creation, genuinely novel products).

1. **Awareness** — surface the problem they haven't named.
2. **Comprehension** — help them understand it and that it applies to them.
3. **Conviction** — proof and stakes that make it undeniable.
4. **Action** — the ask.

### PAPA — Problem, Advantages, Proof, Action

For skeptical audiences, high price points, or trust-poor categories. Same engine as PAS but proof gets its own named stage instead of being implied.

1. **Problem** — as in PAS.
2. **Advantages** — of solving it (life after, concretely).
3. **Proof** — explicit real estate: testimonials, numbers, demos, guarantees. This stage is why PAPA exists; do not compress it.
4. **Action** — the ask, after proof has landed.

### PASOP — email sequences building to a sale

Each email: **P**roblem → **A**gitate → **S**olution → **O**utcome → **new Problem** (the open loop into the next email). The final email is straight PAS ending on the sales link. Every email closes the prior loop and opens the next; the sequence works because no email is self-contained.

## Surface patterns (Axis 1)

### Headlines (page-level)

Build from the hook, as close to verbatim VOC as the surface allows. Pattern shortlist by situation:

- Unfamiliar category/product: `{Product} is a {category} that {thing it does best}`
- Known category, known pain: `The only {category} that doesn't {objection}`
- Outcome + objection in one line: `{Product} {outcome} without {objection}`
- Competitor switching: `{Competitor} {does undesirable thing}. {You} {do desirable thing}.`
- Desire-led: `Here's the {adjective} way to {solve the problem}` / `Get rid of {problem} once and for all`
- Loss-aversion (they tolerate a workaround): `Are you still {wasting resource} on {current approach}?`

Every subhead and crosshead on a page deserves headline-level attention — they are headlines for the section below them.

### Value propositions

- Hero line, few words: **VAD** — Verb + Application + Differentiator ("Drop research directly into blog posts")
- Crowded category: `We do {X}, but the difference is {differentiator}`
- Internal alignment / long-form sections: `For {target} who {need}, {Name} is {category} that {benefit}`

### Subject lines

- Content/nurture default: **Open Loop** — a fraction of the story, paid off in the body. Only works if the body delivers.
- Tighter open-loop variant: **Empty Suitcase** — "this" with no noun ("this is for you")
- Launches/announcements: "Introducing {name}" / "New: {benefit}"
- Deadlines only: scarcity + urgency ("Only {n} days left"). Burns if used routinely.
- Cut-through when the inbox runs long: **The Shorty** — one to three words.

### CTAs / buttons

- Default: start with **"Get"** — low implied effort, positive expectation.
- Alternative: **I Want Button** — complete "I want to ___" from the reader's side and use the completion ("Show me the newest plans"). First person is the mechanism.
- Placement audit: **RAD** — does the reader have what they Require before this button; is it easy to Acquire; do they Desire what it promises? Any "no" means move or rewrite the button, or fix the copy above it.

### Bullets

- Order long lists **BGNGo**: Best first, Good, Necessary, close with Good-with-outcome. Bookend with the two strongest; the middle can sag, the ends can't.
- Write bullets as benefit statements with VOC specificity, not feature labels.

### Testimonials

- Default shape: **Before – After – Experience.** Hesitations before → what they found after → how it felt. Select and lightly trim from real outcome-language VOC (with permission); never write them.
- Short proof between long quotes: keep them Specific, Short, Sizzling, Signed.

### Ads (paid social)

- Body structure: **ERERS** — Emotional, Rational, Emotional, Rational, Social proof. Alternate feeling and justification; close on proof.
- The emotional beats come from VOC problem/desire lines; the rational beats from the offer's concrete mechanics.

### Search ads

Line-by-line: headline attracts with relevance (mirror the query), description line 1 carries the USP, description line 2 asks for the click with the value stated.

## Meta-rules (apply everywhere)

1. **Formulas organize; research supplies.** If a stage has no VOC to fill it, that's a research gap to flag, not a blank to fill with invention.
2. **Don't rush the CTA.** The longest-form direct response puts the ask at step 19 of 21. Match ask-timing to consideration level.
3. **Proof is usually the weakest link.** When in doubt, give proof more explicit space than feels natural.
