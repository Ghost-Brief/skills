# Em Dash Usage Rules

Frequency rule first: em dashes are rationed. Most deliverables should contain zero; a
long-form piece earns at most one or two. Never use them as a default rhythm device. When
one is used, it must serve one of the legitimate functions below (per Merriam-Webster
usage guidance). If a comma, colon, or parenthesis would do the job, prefer those.

## Legitimate functions

1. **Abrupt break or change of direction** in a sentence's structure, including
   interrupted or hesitating speech in dialogue.
2. **Setting off parenthetical or amplifying material** where the aside sits between the
   closeness of commas and the distance of parentheses. Includes defining phrases and
   lists set into a sentence.
3. **Linking clauses in place of a colon or semicolon**, where what follows explains,
   summarizes, or expands the preceding clause.
4. **Setting off illustrative material** introduced by phrases like "for example,"
   "namely," "that is," when the break is stronger than a comma handles.
5. **Introducing a summary statement** after a series of words or phrases.
6. **Attribution of a quotation** (epigraph, extract, blurb) to its author or source.

## Mechanics

- If an em dash falls where a comma could also appear, drop the comma.
- A question mark or exclamation point belonging to material set off by a dash pair goes
  inside the dashes.
- Dashes may appear inside parentheses (and vice versa) for nested parentheticals; omit
  the second dash if it would land immediately before the closing parenthesis.
- En dash (–), not em dash, joins numbers, dates, times, and page ranges meaning "up to
  and including": August 1–August 31, 6:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m., pages 128–34. No "from" or
  "between" needed with an en dash range.
- En dash may replace a hyphen in compound adjectives containing a two-word compound: the
  pre–Websterburg Bakery era.

## The tell vs. the tool

The AI tell is not the em dash itself. It is the reflexive aside, a dash dropped
mid-sentence to create rhythm or fake spontaneity, appearing multiple times per piece. One
structurally earned dash in a long piece is writing; three per paragraph is a fingerprint.

## Judging a specific instance

Ask in order:

1. Would a comma, colon, or parenthesis do this job? If yes, use that instead.
2. Does this dash map to one of the six functions above? If you cannot name which one,
   cut it.
3. Is there already a dash elsewhere in this deliverable? If yes, and this piece is not
   long-form, cut this one.
